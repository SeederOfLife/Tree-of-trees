What the tree shows

The left drives most work and social rights, with peaks in 1936, 1981–82 and 1998–2000. The right and centre carry several major personal freedoms: contraception in 1967, abortion in 1975 and the 1974 opening of the Constitutional Council to opposition MPs.

Most steps back come from regimes without free elections: Napoleon, the restored monarchy and Vichy. Elected majorities undid rights too, such as the 1850 voter purge, the 1920 ban on contraception, the 1960 Mirguet amendment and the pension-age rises of 2010 and 2023.

Rejections matter

Many rights arrived only after being refused first. The Senate blocked votes for women from 1922 until the 1944 ordinance. Parliament rejected abolishing the death penalty in 1791 and again in 1908, before passing it in 1981.

Since 1971 the Constitutional Council also strikes laws down. It did so in 1971 to protect freedom of association, and in 2020 and 2024 on online speech and immigration.

Reading the colours

Camp labels simplify. Ferry's moderate republicans counted as the left in the 1880s but look centrist today, and the Radicals moved from left to centre over the Third Republic. Some laws passed with votes from across parliament. The Veil law relied mostly on left-wing votes, and the 2024 constitutional change passed 780 to 72.

Dates are the date of promulgation for laws, of the vote for referendums and rejected bills, and of the decision for court rulings. Votes are given where a single decisive vote exists.
