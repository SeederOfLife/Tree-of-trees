The evolution of rights in France, 1789–2024

A family tree of French laws and rights. Each branch is a family of rights, each dot is a law, and the dot's colour shows which political camp passed it. Hollow dashed dots are "extinctions", where rights went backwards.

Tap a colour to highlight a camp, tap a row for details.

Branches left to right: democracy · freedoms and equality · work and social · women and family · body and justice

What the tree shows

The left drives most work and social rights, especially in 1936, 1981–82 and 2000. The right and centre are behind several big personal-freedom laws, such as contraception in 1967 and abortion in 1975. Women's rights are spread across almost every camp. All the steps back came from non-democratic regimes: Napoleon, the restored monarchy and Vichy.

A note on the colours

Labelling camps is a simplification. Ferry's "moderate republicans" counted as the left in the 1880s but would look centrist today. Some laws also passed with votes from across parliament: the Veil abortion law relied mostly on left-wing votes, and the 2024 constitutional change got a huge cross-party majority.
